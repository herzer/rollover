Minka: a new approach

You said v2 was an epic fail and asked whether any other approach would not end up so uncanny. This page decides which way Minka gets made from here, and what we make first to test it.

The one assumption this rests on. Worth a yes or no first

In the game, Minka is only ever seen from the board's own fixed view. Players never turn the camera around her. If that holds, she does not have to be a live 3D model at all: she can be filmed (rendered) ahead of time, the way your film was, and the game just plays the clips. If you want her to turn freely or be posed live, the recommended option below does not work, and option 3 or 4 is the way.

Why v2 reads uncanny (my diagnosis)

Your reference · Minka v2 as the game draws her · her face

  • Almost real, but not quite. A photo-like coat and live fur on a face that is not a real cat's face. The closer a character gets to real, the more each small error reads as wrong. That gap is the uncanny valley.
  • Doll eyes. Glossy discs with a hard dot of light, no wet rim, no shadow from the lid. A real cat's eye sits under the lid and catches light softly.
  • A printed face. Her stripes come from the Tripo model's texture, stretched over a different face. Brows and cheeks do not line up with the shapes under them, so the markings read like a decal.
  • Browser fur. Live fur in a web page is layers of fuzz with no clumps or direction. It reads felted, not furry.
  • Two half-good sources. Tripo's AI mesh has a vague face, and the Toon Cats mesh is a cartoon. Neither is a film-quality cat, and combining them did not fix that.

What stays and what goes with the recommended option

Stays

  • The game itself: rules, table, rack, palettes, the kitten joker.
  • Minka's spot on the rack, and the reactions you asked for: walking over the board, swiping tiles, licking her paws.
  • Your Minka references and your eye texture.
  • All 3D files so far, kept in the project (Tripo Minka, toon Minka, v2).

Goes

  • Turning her around live. She is seen as each clip was made.
  • Reacting anywhere on the fly. Each reaction is a clip we make ahead of time, played where it fits (she can still cross the table, since the clip slides across the screen).
  • The live fur and 3D model work, which goes unused (kept, not deleted).

Where she goes

Same place as today's kitten mascot: at the end of your rack. Clips that travel, like walking over the board, move across the table and come back. Nothing else on the screen moves.
